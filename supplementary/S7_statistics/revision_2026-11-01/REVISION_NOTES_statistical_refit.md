## 1. The decision rules applied (quotable in Methods §2.8)

**1.1 Standard errors.**
> Models fitted to the topic-by-year panel use cluster-robust standard errors with the topic as the
> clustering unit, because each of the 25 topics contributes up to 12 repeated yearly observations;
> point estimates are unaffected. Heteroskedasticity-consistent (HC0) standard errors and a GEE fit with
> an exchangeable working correlation within topic are reported as sensitivity analyses. Models fitted
> to the 12-point yearly subfield series, in which each year contributes one observation, retain
> model-based standard errors, with HC0, quasi-Poisson (Pearson-scaled) and NB2 fits reported alongside.
> Topic-level models, in which each of the 26 topics contributes one observation, retain model-based
> standard errors; the dependence that matters there is the appearance of the same paper in several
> topics, which is addressed by the bootstrap in 1.2 rather than by clustering.

**1.2 Topic overlap.**
> The 26 keyword topics are not disjoint. Topic paper counts sum to 85,287 against a 34,595-paper
> subfield (2.47x) and topic benchmark counts to 270 against 157 unique benchmarks (1.72x); 32 of the
> 158 catalogued benchmarks carry no topic tag and are absent from every topic-level analysis.
> Cluster-robust standard errors absorb repeated measures within a topic but not duplication of the same
> paper across topics. Two sensitivity analyses address the latter: (i) each tagged benchmark is assigned
> to a single primary topic, under three rules - its rarest tag, its commonest tag, and a uniform random
> tag over 200 replicates - and the models are refitted; (ii) a non-parametric bootstrap resamples the
> 157 benchmark PMIDs with replacement over 1,000 replicates, rebuilding the topic and topic-year counts
> within each replicate so that a multi-tagged benchmark enters all of its topics together and an
> untagged benchmark enters none, and percentile intervals are reported.
>
> A uniform rescaling of the paper denominators to match the subfield total is deliberately not applied:
> in a log-link model with an offset and an intercept it is absorbed exactly by the intercept and changes
> no reported quantity. Differential overlap across topics is not identifiable from the released
> aggregate PubMed counts, and no paper-level topic assignment is attempted for the denominators.

**1.3 Multiplicity.**
> Inference is organised into three pre-declared families and the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure is applied
> within each: family A, the 17 primary model tests of benchmark coverage and its trend; family B, the 17
> per-year odds ratios in Table 3; family C, the two headline abstract-level trend models of Section 3.3.
> Unadjusted p-values are reported as nominal, with the BH q-value alongside; Bonferroni-adjusted
> p-values are given for the two results whose standing depends on the adjustment chosen. Family
> membership is listed in full in `multiplicity_bh.csv`.

**1.4 Non-significant results.**
> A non-significant test is reported as a failure to detect a change, with the range the interval
> permits, and never as evidence that no change occurred.

**1.5 Denominator.**
> The evaluation-titled corpus is 489 papers: 486 at the 4 October 2026 pull, plus 3 records indexed
> between 5 and 15 October 2026, all three screened and none a methods benchmark. Proportions of the
> corpus use 489; the 2015-2026 regression series uses the 487 papers with a publication year in that
> window, the remaining 2 being published in 2014.

### B-06 - what the verified-fraction null permits

The binomial GLM gives OR 0.9728 per year (0.9119-1.0379), p = 0.404. Over the 11 year-steps from 2015 to
2026 the cumulative change in the odds is 0.74, with an interval of **0.36 to 1.51**. Anchored at the
observed 2015 yield of 4/12 = 33.3%, the data are compatible with a 2026 yield anywhere between **15.3%
and 42.9%**.

Replacement wording: *the share of evaluation-titled papers that are genuine benchmarks showed no
detectable change (odds ratio 0.97 per year, 0.91-1.04, p = 0.40); over the 2015-2026 window this
interval still permits a fall to roughly a third, or a rise to half as much again, of the 2015
classification yield, so the data rule out neither.* The causal clause "not because evaluation work
became less rigorous" is not supported.

### D-07 - absolute counts and the composition shift

| Period | n (sc-omics abstracts) | Positives (any evaluation check) | % (Wilson 95% CI) | Positives (any problem) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015-18 | 129 | **18** | 14.0 (9.0-21.0) | 71 |
| 2019-21 | 240 | **15** | 6.2 (3.8-10.1) | 105 |
| 2022-23 | 229 | **7** | 3.1 (1.5-6.2) | 105 |
| 2024-26 | 391 | **14** | 3.6 (2.1-5.9) | 161 |
| Total | 989 | **54** | 5.5 | 442 |

The single-cell-omics share of the drawn abstracts rises monotonically: 17.3, 14.9, 24.0, 32.2, 38.7,
55.2, 69.1, 75.2, 79.1, 85.3, 87.2, 88.7% for 2015 to 2026.

**Can the composition-shift confound be tested directly with the shipped data? Largely no.** The only
covariates in `problem_coding_2015_2026.csv` are the year, the title, the abstract text, the PubMed
publication types and the single-cell-omics flag itself. Of these:

- abstract length: adjusting leaves the trend at OR 0.799 (0.728-0.877), p = 2.3e-06;
- publication type: adjusting leaves it at OR 0.793 (0.720-0.873), and there are **0 Review-typed
  abstracts** in the 989-abstract sc-omics stratum, so publication type carries almost no information
  about the composition question;
- restricting to the window in which the composition had largely stabilised attenuates the estimate:
  2019-2026 gives OR 0.847 (0.726-0.989), p = 0.035 on 36 positives, and 2021-2026 gives OR 0.859
  (0.681-1.085), p = 0.202 on 26 positives.

No shipped variable measures what actually changed - whether a 2015 single-cell cancer paper and a 2024
one are the same kind of study. The composition shift therefore **cannot be ruled out as a partial
explanation**; it should be stated in §9 as a distinct threat from constant coder bias, with the
attenuation above given as the strongest available evidence either way.

### D-08 - two supporting sensitivity analyses (both favourable)

**(a) Title-query recall.** The numerator is defined by titles containing
`benchmark|comparison|evaluation|assessment`. Measured against the 269-record catalogue, the query
captures **214/269 = 79.6%** overall and **212/267 = 79.4% (95% CI 74.2-83.8)** of records dated
2015-2026, with no detectable time trend: logistic **OR 1.042 per year (0.929-1.170), p = 0.481**.
Per-year recall 2015-2026 (no 2016 records in the catalogue): 1.00, 0.75, 0.67, 0.64, 0.83, 0.75, 0.92,
0.73, 0.68, 0.86, 0.83.

*Honest caveat to print with it:* the catalogue was itself assembled in part from title-based queries, so
this is recall conditional on catalogue membership, not recall against the true benchmark population. It
bounds the time-trend objection, not the absolute level of under-capture.

**(b) Non-single-cell-omics negative control.** Among the 782 non-sc-omics abstracts the paper discards
(39 positives), the evaluation-check share shows **no decline: OR 1.066 per year (0.960-1.185),
p = 0.231**, against **OR 0.808 (0.740-0.882), p = 2.0e-06** in the 989 sc-omics abstracts (54
positives). Median abstract length rises from **1,436 characters (2015-18) to 1,660 (2024-26)**, so
shrinking abstracts cannot explain fewer stated problems; adjusting for abstract length leaves the
sc-omics trend at OR 0.799.

Figure panel: `supporting_sensitivity.png` / `.pdf`. Suggested caption: *(a) Share of the 267 catalogued
benchmarks dated 2015-2026 whose title matches the screening query, by year, with Wilson 95% intervals
and the fitted logistic trend (OR 1.042 per year, 0.929-1.170, p = 0.481); the dotted line is the pooled
79.4%. (b) Share of sampled abstracts stating at least one evaluation-check problem, by year, separately
for abstracts that use single-cell omics data (n = 989, 54 positives) and for those that do not
(n = 782, 39 positives).*

## 4. Not established / not checked

1. **Whether the composition shift explains the stated-problem decline.** No shipped covariate measures
   study type within the single-cell stratum. The restricted-window estimates (OR 0.847 on 2019-2026,
   0.859 on 2021-2026) are suggestive of attenuation but rest on 36 and 26 positives respectively.
2. **The +-0.7% calibration claim in §2.7**, and the examples 442 vs 443 and 7,528 vs 7,556. The
   underlying per-year reconstructed counts are not in the package. Not established.
3. **The subfield calibration error.** NaN in the released log. Not established.
4. **A paper-level (as opposed to benchmark-level) correction for topic overlap.** The denominators are
   aggregate PubMed query counts with no per-paper topic assignment, so differential overlap across
   topics is not identifiable from the shipped data. Only the numerator overlap could be corrected. The
   standard errors on the topic-level models therefore remain optimistic even after the bootstrap.
5. **A random-effects (GLMM) panel fit.** Only GEE with an exchangeable working correlation was run as a
   check on the clustered sandwich; a topic random-intercept Poisson model was not fitted.
6. **Power / minimum detectable effect calculations.** Not computed for any model.
7. **Second-pass coding, catalogue re-coding and the ground-truth catalogue.** Owned by other tracks; not
   touched here, and no quantity in this note depends on re-coding any abstract.
8. **Figures 1-6 and the manuscript text.** Not modified by this track. Figures 1b, 2c, 3 and 4 will need
   regeneration or caption changes to match the corrected intervals and the new denominator; that is a
   downstream task.
